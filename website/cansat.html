01 / MEASURE, SAMPLE, RECOVER

An atmospheric profile with UV.

Our secondary mission now compares UVA and UVB with altitude. Temperature and pressure remain the compulsory primary measurements.

01

Measure

Measure air temperature and pressure after release and during descent. Transmit both to the ground once every second and estimate relative altitude from pressure.

02

Sample UV

The selected SparkFun Mini AS7331 separates UV channels. Save UVA and UVB with altitude, acquisition time and measurement settings to build a local radiation profile.

03

Recover

A bright reusable parachute provides passive recovery. The proposed UV pod sits above the canopy, with its active face higher than fabric, cable and mounting parts.

How will the sensor avoid parachute shading?

The preliminary model places the UV pod above the canopy crown. Its upward optical face has no printed roof or ordinary clear-plastic cover. The pod and wiring are packed inside the CanSat before launch; the mounting and unfolding arrangement still need physical verification.

What can the UV readings tell us?

We will plot UVA and UVB against relative altitude, retaining raw counts and quality flags. Clouds, solar angle, pod tilt and saturation may influence the profile. A short flight may show no resolvable change; the readings do not automatically provide a validated UV Index or ozone concentration.

How does the UV pod connect to the payload?

A signal and power cable follows a shroud line with separate strain relief. A protected UV supply and differential cable interface are proposed. The cable carries no parachute load, and primary measurements take priority if the UV branch fails.

Where can I see the preliminary design?

Read the current PDR, open the Onshape demonstration model, or view the updated system diagram. The model demonstrates the UV arrangement; it is a preliminary design.

CanSat Components

ESP32-S3 is our selected controller family and the SparkFun Mini AS7331 is our selected UV development sensor. Final wiring, calibration and flight integration are still in progress.

SELECTED PLATFORM

ESP32S3MicroPython · two physical boards

ESP32-S3 controllers

The simplified UV mission proposes one flight controller for sensing, logging and telemetry. Python with MicroPython remains our development platform. The final ESP32-S3 board variant depends on packaging and power measurements.

Board specifications ↗

BMP390 Barometric Sensor

The BMP390 pressure sensor supports altitude and pressure measurements during testing and flight, giving the team data for descent profile analysis.

SELECTED DEVELOPMENT SENSOR

AS7331 UV sensor

The SparkFun Mini AS7331 provides separate UV channels on a 25.4 mm by 12.7 mm board. UVA and UVB are our science channels. It uses 3.3 V power and I2C; calibration and a MicroPython driver remain to be completed. Sensor documentation

LoRa telemetry

An external LoRa link will carry atmospheric measurements and flight status to the ground. The exact radio variant, frequency and event configuration are still to be confirmed.

UV pod and recovery

An ASA Aero cradle and TPU85A compliant interface position the sensor above the parachute crown. A small head-mounted attitude sensor is a candidate for recording actual optical-face tilt. The canopy, harness and pod attachment need physical qualification.

Fabrication and Filaments

The initial custom structure and fixtures will be entirely 3D printed in ASA Aero and TPU85A. A reusable fabric parachute, electronics and wiring complete the flight system.

Bambu Lab X2D 3D Printer

The X2D printer manufactures the CanSat structure, fixtures, test pieces, and revised CAD iterations. Fast printing helps the team test fit and strength before finalising a design.

Automatic Material System

The AMS hardware and filament dryers support material handling and conditioning. ASA Aero and soft TPU85A will each use the appropriate drying settings and printer feed path.

ASA Aero

ASA Aero is selected for the initial shell, internal structure and UV sensor cradle. Printed thickness, layer orientation and foaming settings will be assessed on the actual parts.

TPU85A

TPU85A is selected for the initial impact pad, compliant sensor-pod interface and cable strain relief. These flexible parts complement the ASA Aero structure.

Build Workflow

The project moves from model to print to electronics integration, with notebook evidence collected along the way.

1

Design

CAD defines the CanSat body, internal mounts, access points, and recovery interfaces.

2

Print

Print the initial rigid parts in ASA Aero and the cushioning, compliant interfaces and strain relief in TPU85A, using an appropriate feed path for each material.

3

Integrate

Fit the ESP32-S3, primary sensors, UV pod, radio and storage. Keep the UV cable separate from the recovery load path and record the actual sensor configuration.

4

Document

Testing, failures, changes, and final decisions are recorded in the engineering notebook.
