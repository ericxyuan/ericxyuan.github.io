Calculating…

This model estimates idealised single-axis yaw response. Actual descent performance depends on parachute dynamics, external torques, motor characteristics, sensor feedback and actuator saturation.

Model assumptions and equations

The total mass includes the rotor. Body inertia is Ib = (M − mw)kb², and wheel inertia is Iw = f mwrw². A thin rim uses f = 1; a uniform disc uses f = 0.5. Increasing radius changes inertia with its square, but geometry, balance and containment still need verification.

The ideal request uses two equal, opposite torque phases, with required torque 4Ibθ/T². Positive body yaw uses negative motor torque in the first half. Wheel speed is relative to the body; its inertial speed also includes body rotation. With no external torque, (Ib + Iw)ωb + Iwωw,rel stays zero.

Operating limits constrain the actual response. Constant disturbance torque acts in the positive yaw direction when positive, continues after the command ends, and can leave residual rotation. The controller is an open-loop torque schedule; it does not correct attitude errors using feedback. The minimum duration describes an undisturbed rest-to-rest manoeuvre under the configured actuator model.

The body radius of gyration is a lumped estimate, not a CAD-derived inertia tensor. The model omits pitch, roll, suspension restoring moments, wheel-axis offset, bearing friction and structural flexibility. Wheel RPM alone cannot determine performance, and a motor's maximum RPM does not establish its torque capability. The RPM setting is an analysis limit and does not certify rotor safety.
